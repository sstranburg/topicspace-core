10. Methodology & Analytical Framework

Pipeline

  1. Event ingestion & normalization — Events collected from multiple sources and standardized into a common schema.
  2. Semantic embedding — Events embedded in vector space for similarity analysis.
  3. Storm formation — Time-aware clustering groups semantically related events into short-lived "storms."
  4. Lane classification — Events assigned to thematic lanes (tech, market, policy, corporate).
  5. Narrative lineage formation — Related storms across time are grouped into persistent narrative threads.
  6. Macro-phase detection — Lineages are segmented into phases when themes or actor participation shifts.
  7. Ecosystem lineage detection — Cross-actor storms are grouped to reveal multi-company narratives across the tech ecosystem.
  8. Narrative propagation analysis — Event-level edges reveal how narratives spread between actors.
  9. Lead–lag estimation — Average timing differences identify which actors tend to lead or follow narrative shifts.
  10. Retail & community signal overlay — Retail and community discussions are attached to institutional narratives when similarity and actor overlap thresholds are met.

What to be careful about

  • Propagation is not causality — Propagation paths show narrative diffusion patterns across actors but do not imply that one company's actions caused another narrative to emerge.
  • Storm boundaries are approximate — Storm clustering is time- and similarity-based. Closely related discussions may occasionally split across storms during high-volume periods.
  • Ecosystem aggregation may blur distinct stories — Cross-actor storms and ecosystem lineages can merge related but distinct narratives when actor overlap is high.
  • Low-coherence storms often reflect mixed coverage — Some storms contain a blend of technical, market, and commentary articles. These should be interpreted as noisy narrative clusters rather than clean themes.
  • Lane classification is directional, not definitive — Keyword-based lane assignment (tech, market, policy, corporate) improves structural analysis but may under-classify nuanced topics.
  • Long lineages may represent narrative cycles — Large lineages can include multiple thematic phases within a broader macro narrative rather than a single uninterrupted storyline.
  • Retail/community overlays are signal-seeking — Retail or community discussions are attached using similarity thresholds and actor overlap; these should be interpreted as amplification signals rather than independent validation.
  • Human review remains important — Narrative labels and interpretations improve with iteration but should still be treated as analytical summaries rather than authoritative classifications.
